How I work

Small over large
A smaller system is easier to reason about, test, and hand off. I default to it and need a reason to grow past it.
Simple over clever
Clever code is a liability the moment someone other than its author has to change it, including me six months later.
Clear interfaces over clever internals
I spend more design effort on what a function or service exposes than on how it works inside.
Tests that make change safe
A test suite's job is to let me refactor with confidence, not to hit a coverage number.
Common sense over common practice
Best practices are a starting point, not a substitute for understanding the problem in front of me.

Experience

I currently do this work at EDF Renewables under the official title of Solutions Architect, which mostly means I still write code every day and occasionally get to think about the bigger picture too. Day to day that's TypeScript and Python services, Postgres, Docker and Kubernetes, with AWS or GCP underneath depending on the system. Before that, most of my career has been backend and systems engineering across energy, logistics, finance, and government, building and maintaining software that other people depended on to do their jobs.

A representative example: I inherited a handful of Excel add-in applications, each maintained separately with its own copy of the same logic. Instead of patching each one in isolation, I built shared abstractions around Excel.js and the common application plumbing (logging, telemetry, testing) and moved the add-ins onto it one at a time. The result was less code overall, fewer places for the same bug to hide, and features that used to take a week landing in a day.
